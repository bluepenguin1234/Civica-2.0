📊 What the Score Measures
The Civica Score is a single number (0–100) that answers one question: is this town a sound place to buy a home right now? It's built from four weighted dimensions — each scored independently against all 10,267 towns in the dataset. About 51% of every score is resolved at the town level (crime, income, growth, scale); the other ~49% is inherited from the town's parent county (wages, appreciation, climate, migration, permits), because those genuinely operate at a regional scale.

🔢 How Scores Are Calculated
Every metric is converted to a national percentile (0–100) across the full town universe, so towns of all sizes are comparable. Higher is always better — metrics where lower is better (crime, rent burden, flood loss) are inverted before scoring. The four dimension scores are then weighted and summed into the Civica Score.
Score range: 17.1 – 84.9    National mean: 50.0    Std deviation: 9.9    Town-resolved share: ~51%    Universe: incorporated places with ≥ 1,000 residents (CDPs excluded)
Why Civica over Zillow or Redfin?

Zillow tells you what's for sale. Redfin tells you what just sold. Neither tells you whether the town is a sound financial decision. Civica scores the place itself — affordability, economy, safety, growth — using federal data those platforms don't publish, down to the town. And because Civica earns nothing from agents, listings, or advertising, the scores can't be bought.

📐 4 Dimensions
▾
Each metric inside a dimension is scored as a national percentile, so a town is always compared to all 10,267 others — not to an absolute benchmark. (T) marks town-resolved metrics; (C) marks metrics inherited from the parent county. Below is exactly what's in the model and where the data comes from.
🏠 Affordability — 28%
Rent burden (60%, T) — HUD 2BR Fair Market Rent × 12 ÷ ZIP-allocated town income. Lower is better. The core town affordability signal — does income cover rent?
Appreciation quality (40%, C) — FHFA 3-yr avg annual HPI change. Deviation from a healthy 3–7% range is penalized in both directions. There is no home-value level in the model post-Zillow.
💼 Economy — 28%
Wage level (35%, C) — BLS QCEW average annual wage. Higher wages = stronger labor market.
Sector quality (25%, C) — BLS QCEW employment weighted by NAICS sector. Professional and finance score higher; retail and legacy manufacturing lower.
Economic diversity (HHI) (20%, C) — Herfindahl-Hirschman Index across NAICS employment. Lower HHI = more diversified = more resilient.
Town income growth (20%, T) — change in IRS SOI ZIP-allocated town income across two filing years.
🛡️ Safety & Place — 26%
Violent crime / 100k (30%, T) — FBI NIBRS 2024, agencies mapped to places. Towns with no matched agency inherit the county/RUCC-tier rate — flagged, not penalized.
Property crime / 100k (20%, T) — FBI NIBRS burglary, larceny, motor-vehicle theft, arson.
Town scale (20%, T) — national percentile of town population (urban-character proxy).
Amenity density (15%, C) — Census CBP private establishments per 1,000 residents.
Physical risk (15%, C) — FEMA NFIP flood (40%) + NOAA storm (35%) + USFS wildfire (25%), inverted so lower risk scores higher.
📈 Growth — 18%
Town population growth, 5-yr (35%, T) — Census sub-county estimates, 2020→2025.
Town vs. county + momentum (25%, T) — 5-yr growth relative to the county plus recent 1-yr momentum — the town's pure growth "alpha."
Net migration rate (20%, C) — Census Population Estimates: county net migration per 1,000 residents.
In-mover income quality (10%, C) — IRS SOI in-mover AGI ÷ out-mover AGI.
Permit pipeline (10%, C) — Census BPS new units permitted.
🔬 Key Metrics
▾
These metrics are derived by combining two or more federal sources, allocated to the town level. None of them appear on Zillow, Redfin, or any consumer real estate platform. They're the numbers that actually answer whether a town is worth buying into.
Town Income (ZIP-allocated)
DERIVED METRIC
Formula: IRS SOI ZIP-code total AGI ÷ returns, allocated to each place via the 2020 Census ZCTA→place relationship file, weighted by land-area overlap.
Why it matters: No federal source publishes a survey-grade town median income. This address-share approximation varies town-to-town for real — it's how Civica resolves income below the county line. It is labeled as an approximation everywhere it appears, and falls back to county per-capita income for the ~1% of towns with no ZIP allocation.
Rent Burden
DERIVED METRIC
Formula: HUD 2-bedroom Fair Market Rent × 12 ÷ ZIP-allocated town income. Lower is better.
Why it matters: With Zillow gone, this replaces price-to-rent and breakeven as the core affordability signal. Under 25% = local incomes comfortably carry local rents. 25–35% = stretched. Over 35% = affordability is a real constraint, regardless of list prices.
Sector Quality Score
DERIVED METRIC
Formula: Σ(employment share × NAICS quality weight) across BLS QCEW supersectors. Professional/Finance: 1.30×. Healthcare/Education: 1.00×. Retail: 0.60×. Legacy Manufacturing: 0.60×.
Why it matters: Two counties can have identical employment levels but very different economic quality. A county where 40% of workers are in professional services is structurally more resilient than one where 40% are in retail.
Employment Concentration (HHI)
DERIVED METRIC
Formula: Herfindahl-Hirschman Index = Σ(sector employment share²) × 10,000 across NAICS codes. Under 1,500 = diversified. Over 2,500 = concentrated.
Why it matters: A county where 60% of jobs are in one industry is fragile. When that sector contracts, home values follow. Diversification is the housing market equivalent of portfolio risk management.
In-Mover Income Quality
DERIVED METRIC
Formula: IRS SOI in-mover average AGI ÷ IRS SOI out-mover average AGI (2022–2023 migration flows)
Why it matters: Raw migration counts can mislead. A county gaining low-income households while losing high-income ones is deteriorating economically even if net migration is positive. This ratio captures whether the county is becoming more or less affluent over time.
Physical Risk Index
DERIVED METRIC
Formula: NFIP flood claims per capita (40%) + NOAA storm damage per capita (35%) + USFS wildfire rank (25%). All percentile-inverted so lower risk = higher score.
Why it matters: Insurance costs are already rising 20–40% in high-risk counties. This index captures actual realized losses — not modeled probabilities — using the same source hazard data FEMA uses internally to build the National Risk Index.

❓ FAQ
▾
What counts as a "town," and why 10,267 of them?
A town is a US Census incorporated place with at least 1,000 residents — 10,267 of them nationwide. The 1,000-person floor parallels the old county model's 5,000 floor: below it, town income, crime, and growth get too sparse to score honestly. Census Designated Places (CDPs) are excluded for now because the sub-county estimates program doesn't cover them with an annual growth signal.
Why is ~49% of the score inherited from the county?
Some things genuinely operate at a regional scale — average wages, home-price appreciation, climate risk, migration, and permits are county-or-metro phenomena, not town phenomena. Faking them per-town would be dishonest. So Civica resolves what it credibly can at the town level (crime, income, growth, scale ≈ 51%) and clearly labels the rest as county-inherited. Every town report shows the split.
How accurate is town-level income and crime?
Both are real federal data, approximately placed. Town income is IRS ZIP-code AGI allocated to places by land-area overlap — it varies town-to-town for real, but it's an address-share estimate, not a survey median. Crime is FBI NIBRS mapped from each reporting agency's name to a place; county sheriffs (who cover unincorporated area) stay in the county pool, and towns with no matched agency inherit the county/RUCC-tier rate and are flagged — never penalized for not reporting.
Why is there no home price or "price-to-rent" anymore?
Dropping Zillow — the only non-federal source — removed the monthly home-value level the county model used. Rather than fake it, affordability is rebuilt on what federal data does support at the town level: rent burden (HUD 2BR rent vs. town income) plus FHFA appreciation quality. The upshot is that Civica is now 100% federal data.
How often are scores updated?
Scores are recalculated as new federal data releases — Census sub-county estimates and IRS ZIP AGI annually, FHFA/BLS/HUD on their own cadences. Current scores reflect FBI NIBRS 2024, IRS SOI 2021–2022, Census sub-est 2020–2025, and HUD FMR FY2026.
Is this financial advice?
No. Civica scores are for informational and research purposes only. They are not financial, investment, or real estate advice. Real estate decisions involve local factors, individual financial situations, and market conditions that no model fully captures. Always consult a qualified professional before making a purchase decision.
