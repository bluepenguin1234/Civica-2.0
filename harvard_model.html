What separates this model from every real estate website you've used

Zillow tells you what's for sale. Realtor.com tells you what just sold. Neither tells you whether the county is a sound financial decision. Civica's research model answers that question using the same analytical frameworks a housing economist would apply: price-to-rent ratios, real vs. nominal appreciation, supply elasticity, permit gaps, breakeven horizons, and probability-weighted 5-year scenarios.

The model was designed around a simple question: what would a Harvard housing economist want to know before advising someone to buy here?

🔬 8 Derived Metrics — What Civica Computes That Others Don't
These metrics are not in any federal dataset — they are derived by combining two or more sources. They are the metrics that matter most to a sophisticated buyer or investor.
1. Price-to-Rent Ratio
NEW METRIC
Formula: FHFA county HPI index value ÷ (HUD Fair Market Rent × 12)
Why it matters: The most fundamental valuation ratio in real estate. Below 15x = cheap (buy). 15–20x = fair value. Above 20x = expensive, renting may be better. Above 25x = market is pricing in significant future appreciation — high risk if that doesn't materialize. National historical norm: ~15x.
2. Buy vs. Rent Breakeven Horizon
NEW METRIC
Formula: Transaction costs (11%) ÷ (Annual appreciation − Annual ownership premium over rent)
Why it matters: The single most useful number for someone deciding whether to buy now or wait. Under 3 years = strong buy signal. 3–5 years = fair. Over 5 years = renting is likely the better financial choice at current prices.
3. Real (Inflation-Adjusted) Appreciation
NEW METRIC
Formula: FHFA HPI 5yr CAGR − CPI 5yr average
Why it matters: Nominal appreciation of 5% during a 4% inflation environment is only 1% real return. Many counties showing "strong" appreciation are actually losing purchasing power in inflation-adjusted terms. Real appreciation is the honest measure of wealth creation.
4. Permit Gap Ratio
NEW METRIC
Formula: Census Building Permits (annual) ÷ Net new households (Census estimates)
Why it matters: Below 1.0 = supply is not keeping up with demand → prices supported. Above 2.0 = oversupply risk → prices may soften. This is the best predictor of whether current appreciation will persist or reverse within 3–5 years.
5. Real Wage Growth
NEW METRIC
Formula: BLS QCEW average weekly wage 3yr CAGR − CPI 3yr average
Why it matters: Nominal wage growth of 3% during 5% inflation is a declining real wage — the county's residents are getting poorer. Real wage growth is what drives sustainable demand for housing. A county with positive real wage growth is building buyer purchasing power for the future.
6. Employment Concentration Risk
NEW METRIC
Formula: Herfindahl-Hirschman Index across BLS QCEW 4-digit NAICS employment
Why it matters: A county where 60% of employment is in one sector (oil, auto, government) is fragile. When that sector contracts, home values follow. Diversified employment is a structural buffer against single-industry shocks — the housing market equivalent of portfolio diversification.
7. In-Mover Income Quality Score
NEW METRIC
Formula: (IRS SOI avg AGI of in-movers − avg AGI of out-movers) ÷ county median AGI
Why it matters: Raw migration counts can be misleading — a county gaining low-income households while losing high-income ones is deteriorating. This metric captures the quality of demand, not just the quantity. Positive = county is becoming more affluent. Negative = displacement or decline.
8. FEMA Expected Annual Loss per Capita
NEW METRIC
Formula: FEMA NRI Expected Annual Loss ÷ Census population (per capita normalization)
Why it matters: Raw FEMA EAL numbers favor large counties because more people = more expected loss in dollar terms. Per-capita normalization makes risk comparable across counties of all sizes. Combined with the Social Vulnerability Index, it captures both physical exposure and community resilience.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions
Why is price-to-rent ratio weighted so heavily?
It's the cleanest single-number measure of whether a market is over or undervalued. Unlike price-to-income (which tells you about affordability), price-to-rent tells you whether the asset is priced correctly relative to its income-generating potential. A P/R of 9x like Wayne County, MI looks like a bargain — but paired with negative real appreciation and out-migration, it signals structural decline, not undervaluation. The Research Model surfaces both pieces together.
Why is the breakeven horizon sometimes short even when P/R is high?
Because breakeven is driven by the spread between appreciation and the ownership premium over renting — not by P/R alone. A high-P/R county with very strong appreciation can still have a short breakeven horizon if prices are rising fast enough to offset the extra cost of owning vs. renting. Jefferson County (P/R 22.2x, breakeven 2.1 years) is an example. The risk is that if appreciation slows, the breakeven extends rapidly.
What's the permit gap ratio and why does it matter more than vacancy rates?
Vacancy rates tell you the current supply-demand balance. Permit gap tells you the trajectory — whether the imbalance is getting worse or better. A county with tight vacancy but a high permit ratio (lots of new construction coming) may see prices soften in 2–3 years. A county with a low permit ratio has structural undersupply that tends to persist for years because supply response (permitting, approvals, construction) is inherently slow. The permit gap is a leading indicator where vacancy is a lagging one.
How are the scenario probabilities determined?
Scenarios are based on qualitative assessment of the county's macro exposure, not statistical forecasting models. Bull probability increases with low P/R, high supply constraint, strong migration quality, and diversified economy. Bear probability increases with high P/R, rate sensitivity (high payment-to-income), single-employer concentration, and elevated physical risk. Probabilities are illustrative — not the output of a calibrated quantitative model.
Why does the Research Model weight Population Momentum at only 6%?
Migration is the sharpest signal when it's strong — but it's also the most volatile. The 2020–2022 remote work migration to Sun Belt and Mountain West counties was real, but some of it is reversing. Weighting migration too heavily would cause the model to over-score counties whose demand surge was driven by a transient factor. The Research Model lets Economic Vitality (wages, jobs, income growth) and Housing Market Dynamics (supply constraint) carry more weight because they are more structurally durable.
