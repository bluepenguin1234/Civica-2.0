📊 What the Score Measures
The Civica Score is a single number (0–100) that answers one question: is this county a sound place to buy a home right now? It's built from six weighted dimensions — each scored independently against all 2,820 counties in the dataset.

🔢 How Scores Are Calculated
Every metric is converted to a national percentile (0–100) so counties of all sizes are comparable. Higher is always better — metrics where lower is better (like crime rate or flood loss) are inverted before scoring. The six dimension scores are then weighted and summed into the Civica Score.

Why Civica over Zillow or Redfin?

Zillow tells you what's for sale. Redfin tells you what just sold. Neither tells you whether the county is a sound financial decision. Civica scores the market itself — affordability, economic strength, risk — using data those platforms don't publish. And because Civica earns nothing from agents, listings, or advertising, the scores can't be bought.

🎯 The 5 Civica Signals
Every county gets one signal. It combines the strength of local fundamentals (Civica Score) with current market direction (HPI momentum vs. 3-year average + net migration rate). The result is one plain-English answer to "should I buy here right now?"
STRONG BUY
All signals firing
Top-tier fundamentals — strong economy, defensible prices, low risk, people moving in — and the market is actively improving. The highest-conviction entry point in the model.
Score ≥ 57 + market improving  ·  ~75 counties (2.7%)
BUY
Good fundamentals
Either a strong market holding steady, or a solid market actively improving. The data supports buying with confidence — this is where most well-researched purchases land.
Score ≥ 57 + stable, or score 45–56 + improving  ·  ~629 counties (22%)
WATCH
Mixed signals
Market conditions are mixed or in transition. Might be right for your situation — but don't rush. Monitor for a quarter before committing. The largest bucket by design.
Varies by score + direction  ·  ~1,199 counties (42%)
CAUTION
Weakening
Fundamentals are below average or softening. Higher risk of near-term value erosion. Requires a strong personal or financial reason to buy here now — the data isn't on your side.
Score 45–56 + declining, or score 37–44 + stable  ·  ~706 counties (25%)
AVOID
Poor fundamentals
Multiple dimensions are underperforming with no meaningful positive trend. Unless you have a strong non-financial reason (family, job), your capital works harder somewhere else.
Score < 37, or score 37–44 + declining  ·  ~211 counties (7.5%)
How direction is measured: Market direction uses two signals from the data already in the model: (1) whether the latest FHFA HPI annual change is more than 1 percentage point above or below the county's 3-year average, and (2) whether the Census net migration rate is above or below ±1.0 per 1,000 residents. A county scores +1 or −1 on each. Sum ≥ 1 = improving; sum ≤ −1 = declining; otherwise stable. This is combined with the Civica Score tier to produce the final signal.
📐 What Each Dimension Actually Measures
Each metric inside a dimension is scored as a national percentile, so a county is always compared to all 2,820 others — not to an absolute benchmark. Below is what's actually in the model and where the data comes from.
💰 Affordability & Value — 25%
Price-to-rent ratio (30%) — Zillow ZHVI ÷ HUD Fair Market Rent × 12. National norm: 15–18x. The core valuation signal.
Price-to-income ratio (30%) — Zillow ZHVI ÷ BEA per capita income. Uses per-capita income, so ratios appear higher than the commonly cited 4.2× norm (which uses household income).
Buy vs. rent breakeven (25%) — Down payment (20%) ÷ (monthly PITI − HUD 2BR FMR) × 12. Assumes 7% 30-yr fixed, 1.2% property tax, 0.5% insurance. Shorter is better.
Appreciation quality (15%) — FHFA 3-yr avg annual HPI change. Deviation from a 3–7% healthy range is penalized in both directions.
📈 Economic Vitality — 22%
Wage level (35%) — BLS QCEW average annual wage. Higher wages = stronger labor market and more purchasing power.
Sector quality (25%) — BLS QCEW employment weighted by NAICS sector. Professional and finance sectors score higher; retail and legacy manufacturing score lower.
Economic diversity (HHI) (25%) — Herfindahl-Hirschman Index across NAICS employment. A county dominated by one sector is fragile; lower HHI = more diversified.
Per-capita income growth (15%) — BEA CAINC1 4-year growth. Rising real incomes drive sustainable housing demand.
🏗️ Market Dynamics — 20%
3-year appreciation trend (35%) — FHFA HPI 3-yr average annual change. Sustained appreciation signals genuine underlying demand.
Current momentum (15%) — FHFA HPI latest annual change. Cross-validates the trend signal using a different time window.
Inventory tightness (30%) — Zillow active listing count (percentile-inverted). Lower inventory relative to other counties = tighter market.
Permit pipeline (20%) — Census BPS new units permitted. Measures whether supply is responding to demand.
🏡 Quality of Place — 15%
Violent crime rate (35%) — FBI NIBRS 2024 violent offenses per 100k. Counties without NIBRS coverage receive their RUCC-tier median — rural non-reporters are not penalized for not reporting.
Urban access (40%) — USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Code 2023. Scores the RUCC 1–9 continuum so metro counties score higher than remote rural.
Amenity density (25%) — Census CBP private establishments per 1,000 residents. More services per capita = stronger local commercial base.
🌊 Physical Risk — 12%
Flood loss proxy (40%) — FEMA NFIP paid claims ÷ Census population (10-year average). Captures actual insured flood losses per capita.
Storm damage proxy (35%) — NOAA Storm Events property damage ÷ population (5-year average). Covers all storm types beyond flood.
Wildfire exposure (25%) — USFS Wildfire Risk to Communities county-level exposure index.
🧭 Population Momentum — 6%
Net migration rate (60%) — Census Population Estimates RNETMIG2023: net migration per 1,000 residents.
In-mover income quality (40%) — IRS SOI in-mover average AGI ÷ out-mover average AGI. A ratio >1.0 means higher-income people are arriving than leaving — a quality-of-demand signal.
🔬 Metrics Civica Computes That Others Don't
These metrics are derived by combining two or more federal sources. None of them appear on Zillow, Redfin, or any consumer real estate platform. They're the numbers that actually answer whether a market is worth buying into.
Price-to-Rent Ratio
PROPRIETARY
Formula: Zillow ZHVI ÷ (HUD Fair Market Rent × 12)
Why it matters: The single most useful valuation check in real estate. Under 15× = buying is clearly cheaper than renting. 15–20× = fair value. Over 20× = you're paying a premium to own. Over 25× = the market is pricing in significant future appreciation that may not materialize.
Buy vs. Rent Breakeven
PROPRIETARY
Formula: Down payment (20%) ÷ (monthly PITI − HUD 2BR FMR) × 12. Assumes 7% 30-yr fixed, 1.2% property tax, 0.5% insurance. Capped at 30 years.
Why it matters: The most practical number for a buyer deciding whether to buy now or keep renting. Under 3 years = strong case for buying. 3–6 years = reasonable. Over 6 years = renting is likely the better financial choice at current prices.
Sector Quality Score
PROPRIETARY
Formula: Σ(employment share × NAICS quality weight) across BLS QCEW supersectors. Professional/Finance: 1.30×. Healthcare/Education: 1.00×. Retail: 0.60×. Legacy Manufacturing: 0.60×.
Why it matters: Two counties can have identical employment levels but very different economic quality. A county where 40% of workers are in professional services is structurally more resilient than one where 40% are in retail.
Employment Concentration (HHI)
PROPRIETARY
Formula: Herfindahl-Hirschman Index = Σ(sector employment share²) × 10,000 across NAICS codes. Under 1,500 = diversified. Over 2,500 = concentrated.
Why it matters: A county where 60% of jobs are in one industry is fragile. When that sector contracts, home values follow. Diversification is the housing market equivalent of portfolio risk management.
In-Mover Income Quality
PROPRIETARY
Formula: IRS SOI in-mover average AGI ÷ IRS SOI out-mover average AGI (2022–2023 migration flows)
Why it matters: Raw migration counts can mislead. A county gaining low-income households while losing high-income ones is deteriorating economically even if net migration is positive. This ratio captures whether the county is becoming more or less affluent over time.
Physical Risk Index
PROPRIETARY
Formula: NFIP flood claims per capita (40%) + NOAA storm damage per capita (35%) + USFS wildfire rank (25%). All percentile-inverted so lower risk = higher score.
Why it matters: Insurance costs are already rising 20–40% in high-risk counties. This index captures actual realized losses — not modeled probabilities — using the same source hazard data FEMA uses internally to build the National Risk Index.

❓ Common Questions
Why are only 2,820 counties scored, not all 3,143?
The 324 excluded counties all have population under 5,000. Counties that small have no Zillow data, suppressed QCEW employment figures, and often no FHFA HPI history. Their scores would be entirely imputed medians — not real signal. If you search a county under 5,000 residents, Civica will tell you why it's excluded rather than show a misleading score.
Why does my county's price-to-income ratio look higher than what I've seen elsewhere?
Civica uses BEA per-capita personal income (~$67k national average) rather than median household income (~$80k). The commonly cited "4.2× historical norm" uses household income. Using per-capita income, the historical norm is roughly 2.5–3.0×. All counties use the same base, so the scoring is internally consistent — but the displayed P/I ratios will appear 1.5–1.8× higher than industry standard benchmarks. County report pages include a footnote explaining this.
Why does Population Momentum only get 6% of the score?
Migration is the sharpest signal when it's strong — but it's also the most volatile. The 2020–2022 remote work surge to Sun Belt counties was real, and some of it has already reversed. Weighting migration too heavily would cause the model to over-score counties whose demand surge was driven by a transient factor. Economic Vitality and Market Dynamics carry more weight because they reflect structural conditions that are harder to reverse quickly.
Why is the AVOID label at zero counties?
The FBI NIBRS crime integration raised the score floor. The worst-performing county now scores 26.9, which is above the AVOID threshold of 24. The 4 counties labeled SPECULATIVE (scoring 24–29) are the genuine worst performers in the model. This threshold will be reviewed in the next scoring engine update.
How often are scores updated?
Scores are recalculated quarterly as new FHFA, BLS, and IRS data releases. Current scores reflect Q1 2026 data. BLS QCEW reflects 2023 annual data (18-month publication lag). FHFA HPI is through Q4 2025. FBI NIBRS is the 2024 National Master File.
Is this financial advice?
No. Civica scores are for informational and research purposes only. They are not financial, investment, or real estate advice. Real estate decisions involve local factors, individual financial situations, and market conditions that no model fully captures.
