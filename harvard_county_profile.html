✅ Bull Case — Why This Works
1.
Supply cannot catch up. Rocky Mountain geography constrains westward expansion. Permit issuance (3,200/yr) is running 44% below household formation (5,700/yr). Undersupply is structural, not cyclical.
2.
Buyers are getting wealthier, not poorer. IRS data shows in-movers earn $16,800/yr more than those leaving. Demand quality is rising, not just quantity.
3.
Real appreciation is genuine. Inflation-adjusted 5-year CAGR of +3.7%/yr means buyers are building real wealth, not just keeping pace with inflation.
4.
Short breakeven horizon. At current appreciation and rent levels, buying beats renting in 2.1 years — well below the 4-year threshold that signals overvaluation.
⚠️ Bear Case — What Could Go Wrong
1.
Rate shock. If 30yr mortgage rates rise above 8.5%, monthly cost increases ~$400/mo. At that level, buy vs. rent breakeven extends to 4.8 years — approaching overvalued territory.
2.
Remote work reversal. A significant portion of Jefferson County's 2020–2023 in-migration was driven by remote work flexibility. A broad return-to-office mandate in tech and finance could reduce demand from out-of-state buyers.
3.
Wildfire-driven insurance crisis. If wildfire risk increases further, insurance carriers may exit the market as they have in parts of California. This would increase carrying costs beyond current estimates.
🎓
Civica Research Verdict: Jefferson County is a structurally sound long-term buy. Constrained supply, rising income quality of in-movers, positive real appreciation, and a 2.1-year breakeven horizon justify entry at current prices for buyers with a 3+ year hold horizon. The primary risk is interest rate sensitivity — model your personal breakeven at any rate above 7.5%.
